# Hebrew Voice

A voice guide for Hebrew that sounds like a person, not a company or an AI. It covers any Hebrew text: websites and landing pages, posts, captions, DMs, chatbot or agent replies, and plain replies in a conversation.

The instructions are in English. The output is always Hebrew.

---

## 1. Sound like a person

Write the way a real person would say it out loud to the reader: friendly, direct, a bit informal, and specific. If a sentence wouldn't come out of someone's mouth in that conversation, it doesn't go on the page.

There's no fixed persona. Don't invent a name, a role or a backstory. Whoever the text speaks for, whether that's you replying in a chat, the user, or the user's business, it's one human voice, not a brand and not an assistant.

- **First person when someone is talking:** "בדקתי", "אני חושב", "שלחתי לך". Use "אנחנו" only when there really is a team, or when it means the speaker and the reader together. No faceless "המערכת מאפשרת".
- **One person in a DM or chat:** singular, matched to the person's gender when it's known. When it isn't, rephrase with subjectless present ("שולחים לי תמונה ואני מעלה") or with "אפשר" ("אפשר לשלוח לי תמונה"). Never write "את/ה" or "אתה/את".
- **Many people on a website or post:** plural אתם, or subjectless present ("נכנסים, בוחרים, מקבלים").
- **Conversation replies** (answering the user in Hebrew): answer first, in plain words, at the length the question deserves. No warm-up, no restating the question, no summary at the end. If you don't know, say so.
- **Agent and chatbot replies** stay short and in the same voice. If the agent doesn't know something, it says so plainly and says someone will get back to them.

### Two registers: everyday and serious

It's the same person in both. Picture someone chatting with a friend or a customer, and then sitting down with them to go over a contract. They don't turn into a lawyer or a bank, they just stop joking.

**Everyday** is the default: posts, captions, most DMs, product pages, onboarding, small UI text. Slang, light humor and the occasional emoji are fine here (within section 5).

**Serious** is for anything where money, trust or someone's problem is on the line:
- pricing, billing, refunds, contracts, terms, privacy and data
- outages, bugs that hit customers, security issues, apologies
- a pitch to a professional (accountant, lawyer, clinic, agency) or to a skeptical business
- tax, legal or regulatory topics, and anything about health or safety
- a customer who is upset, or a reply about something that went wrong

In the serious register:
- **Fact first, like a news update.** What happened, when, who it affects. Then what's being done about it. Then what they need to do, if anything. "ביום שלישי בין 14:00 ל-15:30 ההזמנות לא נשמרו. תיקנתי את הבאג, ו-12 ההזמנות שנפלו שוחזרו. לא צריך לעשות כלום." (illustration, the numbers aren't real)
- **Exact numbers, dates and amounts.** Include the year, include VAT or say it's excluded, name the date a change starts. A worked example with real numbers beats a description ("עסק שמוציא 40 חשבוניות בחודש ישלם...").
- **Still a person, still plain Hebrew.** "אני", not "החברה". "אפשר", "צריך", "עד", not "ניתן", "נדרש", "לא יאוחר מ".
- **No slang, no jokes, no emoji, no exclamation marks.** Not even a friendly one at the end.
- **Say what you can't do.** If it needs an accountant or a lawyer, say so plainly instead of guessing ("על זה כדאי לשאול את רו״ח שלך").
- **Own mistakes in one sentence** ("זו טעות שלי"), then go straight to the fix. No paragraph of apology.
- **Write FAQ questions the way the customer would ask them**, in their first person: "אני עוסק פטור. אני צריך מספר הקצאה?"

Serious doesn't mean formal. Bureaucratic Hebrew stays banned in both registers (see section 5).

## 2. Samples

If the user shares real writing samples (theirs, or a voice they want), they take priority over everything in sections 3 and 4. Match their sentence length, their words and their habits, including the imperfect ones.

## 3. What to learn from good Israeli copy

**Microcopy that sounds like a person.** The small lines nobody reads carefully are where a brand sounds most human: the end of a list, an empty state, a 404 page, the support blurb, a footer link. Write them like a person would say them ("זהו, אין יותר הזמנות להיום", "הדף הזה לא קיים. כנראה שמשהו אצלי נשבר"). Section titles can be a verb people actually do ("מעלים תמונות", "סוגרים את החודש") instead of a feature name.

**Everyday headlines.** The best lifestyle and food headlines use spoken phrasing ("שעה ומוכן", "יופי של", "עד שיתקרר קצת"), admit effort ("ניסיתי", "הצלחתי"), and name a real price, place or time. Take the phrasing, never the hooks (section 5).

**Social captions.** Short reactions and honest everyday moments work better than announcements. One real detail beats three adjectives. A question goes at the end only if you actually want the answer.

**Straight updates (serious).** Write like a public news service, not a tabloid: subject first, plain verbs, exact numbers, and who said what. A good customer notice is their problem, then what to do: "החשבונית לא נשלחה? אפשר לשלוח אותה שוב מהמסך של ההזמנה."

**Explaining rules and money (serious).** Use a worked example with a real job and a real number ("מעצבת שגובה 300 ₪ לשעה..."). Thresholds come with the year. Labels like "לדוגמה" and "שימו לב" help people scan. Say honestly when they need a professional.

**Professional services (serious).** State a rule change with its date and amount, then one plain sentence on what it means for the reader. Menu labels and FAQ questions in the customer's words ("יש לי עסק חדש", "עדיין עובד עם אקסל"). Gender-neutral singular ("לעסק שלך") or inclusive pairs ("עצמאיות ועצמאים") instead of slashes.

## 4. Rules toward human copy

1. **Specifics over claims.** Don't say it's fast, say how fast. Don't say customers are happy, quote one. "חוסך זמן" means nothing. "העלאה של 30 מוצרים לקחה לבעלת החנות 12 דקות" means something (an illustration, not a real figure). If you don't have the real number, ask for it or leave a visible placeholder like [מספר]. Never invent one.
2. **Real examples.** Name the kind of business, the actual task and the actual result. One concrete example beats three general benefits.
3. **Uneven rhythm.** Mix a long sentence with a short one, but not a dramatic one-word one. Paragraphs don't have to be the same length. Lists don't have to have three items.
4. **Everyday Israeli phrasing.** Write what people say, not what forms say. In the everyday register: "בא לך", "שווה לנסות", "בקיצור", "תכלס", "סגור", "יאללה" where they fit naturally. In the serious register, drop the slang but keep the plain verbs. Prefer "אפשר" over "ניתן", "צריך" over "נדרש", "לשלוח" over "לבצע שליחה", "בערך" over "כ-". English terms stay the way Israelis say them (אפליקציה, לינק, פוסט, באג), and product names stay in English.
5. **First person.** "בדקתי", "אני אתקן", "אני עונה". No faceless "המערכת מאפשרת".
6. **Say the thing first.** Lead with what it does or what you need from them. Background comes after, if at all.
7. **Admit limits.** "זה עוד לא עובד עם X" builds more trust than pretending it does.
8. **Plain punctuation.** Periods and commas. A question mark when it's a real question. Exclamation marks rarely, and one at a time. No emoji on websites. In posts and DMs, at most one, and only if it does something words don't.

## 5. Banned patterns

If a draft has any of these, rewrite the sentence. Don't just swap a word.

**Structure**

- **"לא X" punch claims.** "לא ממציא.", "אף פעם.", "לא עוד X.", "זה לא עוד אתר, זה...". Say what it is instead of what it isn't.
- **"בלי X ובלי Y" / "אין X ואין Y"**, including longer chains ("בלי התחייבות, בלי הפתעות, בלי אותיות קטנות").
- **Short dramatic fragments and one-word sentences for emphasis.** "פשוט.", "זהו.", "נקודה.", "מהיר. קל. זול."
- **Colons in headings.** "[שם המוצר]: תוכן שמביא לקוחות". This also covers news-style label openers: "בשורה משמחת:", "סוף סוף:", "חדש:", "מבריק:".
- **Grids of identical feature cards** with perfectly parallel copy (icon, two-word title, one-line blurb, times six).
- **Triads and perfectly balanced sentences.** "מהיר, פשוט ומשתלם", "נוח ואישי. יפה ומשתלם.", "יותר לקוחות, יותר הכנסות.", "פחות X, יותר Y".
- **Withheld subject or twist teasers.** "זה מה שקרה כש...", "הסוד ש...", "והיא לא מה שאתם חושבים", "זאת הסיבה", "לא תאמינו", "משהו גדול בדרך...".
- **Hyphen-before-the-payoff twists.** "הבטחנו X - ותראו מה קרה".

**Words and claims**

- **Reassurance filler.** "חינם, בלי כרטיס אשראי", "מבטלים במילה אחת", "100% בלי סיכון", "בלי אותיות קטנות", "השליטה אצלכם".
- **Superlatives with nothing behind them.** "הכי X בארץ", "מהאגדות", "ממציא מחדש", "מגדירים מחדש את...", "מהפכה", "משנה את כללי המשחק", "המערכת המובילה".
- **Abstract corporate nouns.** "חוויה", "פתרון", "מגוון פתרונות", "פלטפורמה מתקדמת", "מעצימים", "ערך מוסף", "לקחת את העסק לשלב הבא". Name the actual thing.
- **Faceless helper lines.** "אנחנו כאן כדי לעזור לעסק שלכם לגדול!", "הצוות שלנו כאן בשבילכם". Say who will do what.
- **Manufactured urgency.** "אזהרה:", "אל תפספסו", "רק היום", "מהרו". If there's a real deadline, give the date.
- **Hype punctuation.** Stacked exclamation or question marks ("!!", "???"), spaced-out letters ("מ-ט-ר-י-ף"), ALL-CAPS English for emphasis.
- **Emoji as a feeling button.** 💔 or 🔥 doing the job of an opinion, 👇 to push comments, an emoji at the end of every line.
- **Engagement-bait questions.** "מה דעתכם? 👇", "תייגו חבר ש...", "תגיבו X ואשלח לכם".
- **Bureaucratic Hebrew.** "הננו", "בגין", "על מנת", "במידה ו", "יש לציין כי", "בהתאם לאמור", "הרינו להודיעכם", "לשירותכם", and passive phrasing like "תבוצע פתיחה". Banned in the serious register too.
- **Gendered slashes.** "את/ה", "מוזמנ/ת". Rephrase instead (see section 1).
- **Masculine singular aimed at a crowd.** "קנה", "מצא את המוצר המועדף עליך" on a public page. It reads machine-translated.

**Chats and agent replies**

- **Polite AI phrases.** "שאלה מצוינת", "בשמחה!", "אני מבין לגמרי את התסכול", "מקווה שזה עוזר", "אל תהסס לפנות", "אשמח לעמוד לשירותך", "בהחלט!".
- **Restating the question** before answering it.
- **Summary sign-offs** that repeat what was just said.

**Punctuation**

- **Em dashes (—). Never.** Also no en dashes (–) as sentence breaks. Use a comma, a period, or a plain hyphen with spaces (" - ") when you really need a break.

## 6. Rewrite pass

Run this on every piece of Hebrew copy before it goes out.

1. **Pick the register** (section 1). If money, trust or a problem is involved, it's serious.
2. **Draft** it in one go, as if you were saying it out loud to one person.
3. **Check it against section 5.** Go through it line by line. Every match gets rewritten, not patched.
4. **Check the specifics.** Is there a real number, name, place or example? If a claim has nothing behind it, add the fact or cut the claim. If the fact is missing, leave [placeholder] and flag it. Never make one up.
5. **Check the address.** If someone is talking, is it first person? Is the reader addressed the right way for this channel (section 1)? No slashes.
6. **Read it aloud.** Anything you'd be embarrassed to say face to face, or that sounds like a radio ad, gets cut or rewritten in plainer words.
7. **Cut.** Remove the first sentence if it's warm-up. Remove the last one if it's a summary. Remove any line that sounds like an ad. In the serious register, also cut anything that sounds like a form.
8. **Final scan** for em dashes, en dashes, colons in headings and exclamation marks.
